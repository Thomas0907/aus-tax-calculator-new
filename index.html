2025–26 Financial Year Guide

The Complete 2025–26 Guide to Working Holiday Maker (417/462) Tax Refunds in Australia

Reviewed against current ATO guidance for the 2025–26 financial year (1 July 2025 – 30 June 2026). This article is general information only and is not personal tax advice — see the Legal Tax Disclaimer at the bottom of this page.

If you're on a subclass 417 or 462 visa and trying to work out how much tax you'll pay — or get back — this guide walks through the exact rates, the residency question that trips up almost everyone, legitimate ways to boost your refund, and a step-by-step walkthrough of lodging through myGov.

Understanding WHM Tax Brackets for the 2025/2026 Financial Year

Australia introduced a dedicated tax schedule for working holiday makers in 2017, commonly known as the "backpacker tax." Unlike Australian tax residents, visa holders on 417 and 462 are taxed at 15% for the first $45,000 of their income, with anything above that taxed at 32.5%, and higher foreign-resident rates apply beyond that.

The single biggest thing to understand is this: working holiday maker tax rates for 2025–26 and 2026–27 start at a flat 15% on the first $45,000 of income for subclass 417 and 462 visa holders, and this applies regardless of whether you are classified as an Australian tax resident or a foreign resident for tax purposes. This is different from ordinary residents, who enjoy the first $18,200 completely tax-free — WHM visa holders don't get that concession at all.

The 15% rate only applies correctly if your employer has done their part. Employers hiring working holiday makers must register with the ATO to withhold at the 15% rate — if they haven't, they're required to withhold at foreign-resident rates, which is 30% from the very first dollar. It's worth asking your employer directly, or checking your payslip, to confirm this registration has happened — it's one of the most common reasons WHM workers end up owed a refund.

A worked example from the ATO illustrates how this plays out in practice: when a registered employer pays a WHM worker weekly, tax is withheld at 15% from the very first pay — for instance, a $1,000 weekly pay results in $150 withheld. Multiply that across the year and you can see how quickly the tax withheld adds up, which is exactly why lodging a return to check the final numbers matters.

One more thing to flag: without a Tax File Number, the situation gets much worse. Employers must withhold 45% from payments to a working holiday maker who has not provided a TFN within 28 days of starting — so applying for your TFN before your first pay cycle should be one of the very first things you do after arriving.

Are You a Tax Resident or Working Holiday Maker? DASP and Residency Explained

This is where most WHM visa holders get confused, so let's separate two different questions that people often mix up: your tax residency status, and your WHM visa status. They interact, but they don't cancel each other out.

Residency for WHM purposes doesn't change your 15% rate in most cases. For most WHMs, whether you're an Australian or a foreign resident for tax purposes doesn't affect the rate of tax you pay — the special WHM schedule applies either way. The exception involves specific "non-discrimination article" (NDA) countries under Australia's tax treaties, where a landmark High Court case changed things.

The Addy case matters here. In Addy v Commissioner of Taxation [2021] HCA 34, the High Court found that applying the backpacker tax to a UK working holiday maker who was also an Australian tax resident breached the non-discrimination article of the Australia–UK double tax treaty. In practical terms: if you're from a treaty country with a non-discrimination clause (the UK, US, Germany, Japan, Chile, Finland, Israel, Norway, and Turkey are the commonly cited NDA countries) and you meet the standard residency tests, you may be entitled to be taxed at ordinary resident rates — including the $18,200 tax-free threshold — instead of the flat 15%. This is a genuinely complex area and is one of the few situations in this guide where getting registered tax agent advice before lodging is strongly worthwhile.

For everyone else, the standard residency tests (the resides test, the domicile test, the 183-day test, and the superannuation test) mostly determine other things — like whether the Medicare levy could ever apply to you, and whether foreign-sourced income needs to be declared — rather than which WHM rate you pay.

Superannuation and DASP. Superannuation works differently from income tax entirely. All employers must contribute 12% of your salary into a super fund on top of your pay, as of 1 July 2025, and as a WHV holder you can't access this super until you leave Australia permanently. Once your visa has expired and you've departed, you can apply for a Departing Australia Superannuation Payment (DASP). The catch: this isn't taxed the same as your income was. When claiming DASP, the ATO deducts 65% tax and refunds only 35% of the balance to you — a rate that has applied since 1 July 2017. This 65% rate is significantly higher than the 15% income tax rate, so it's worth planning your departure timing accordingly.

How to Maximise Your Tax Refund Legally

Because the 15% rate applies from your first dollar with no tax-free threshold, deductions are one of the few levers you control. Every dollar of legitimate work-related expense reduces your taxable income, which — at the 15% rate — is worth 15 cents back in your pocket per dollar claimed. Here are five categories genuinely relevant to WHM workers:

  1. Work-related travel between job sites. If you work multiple casual jobs or travel directly between two different worksites on the same day (not your regular commute from home), that travel can generally be claimed. Keep a simple log of dates, kilometres, and purpose.
  2. Protective clothing, uniforms, and safety equipment. Farm work, hospitality, and construction-adjacent roles often require specific safety boots, high-vis clothing, gloves, or sun protection gear that isn't reimbursed by the employer. Keep the receipts — a backpacker claiming $500 in legitimate work-related deductions reduces their taxable income by $500, meaning $75 less tax at the 15% rate.
  3. Tools and equipment for your specific role. If you're required to supply your own basic tools (secateurs, a knife kit for kitchen work, etc.), the cost — or depreciation on higher-value items — can often be claimed.
  4. Self-education directly connected to your current job. Short courses or certifications that directly relate to the work you're currently doing (for example, a barista course while working as a barista, or an RSA refresher for hospitality) may be deductible — but a course for a completely different future career generally isn't.
  5. Union or professional association fees, if you're a member of one relevant to your current employment, along with tax agent fees from lodging a previous year's return.

The golden rule: you can only claim expenses you actually incurred, that directly relate to earning your income, and that you have a record for. The ATO's data-matching systems are increasingly sophisticated, and overclaiming can trigger a review — so "maximising" your refund should always mean claiming everything you're legitimately entitled to, not inventing expenses.

Step-by-Step Guide to Lodging Your Tax Return via myGov

You can lodge your own tax return for free directly through myGov, linked to the ATO. Here's the process from start to finish:

  1. Create or log in to your myGov account. Go to my.gov.au and either create an account or log in to your existing one.
  2. Link the ATO to your myGov account. Under "Services," select "Link a service" and choose the Australian Taxation Office. You'll need some identifying details (TFN, bank account details, or an income statement) to verify the link.
  3. Wait for your income statement to be marked "Tax ready." Your employer reports your wages and tax withheld directly to the ATO through Single Touch Payroll. This information usually appears in your myGov account within a few weeks of the financial year ending (30 June). It's worth waiting for this status before lodging, since amending a return afterward is more hassle than a short delay.
  4. Start your tax return. From the ATO online services homepage, select "Tax," then "Lodgments," then "Income tax," and click "Prepare" next to the relevant income year.
  5. Check your pre-filled information. Your income and tax withheld should pre-fill automatically from your income statement(s). If you had more than one employer, confirm all of them appear.
  6. Add your deductions. This is where you manually enter the work-related expenses discussed above, under "Deductions."
  7. Review, declare, and submit. The system will calculate your estimated refund (or amount owing) before you submit. Read through the summary carefully, confirm the declaration, and lodge.
  8. Track your refund. Most electronically lodged returns are processed within about two weeks, and you can track progress under "Manage tax returns" in ATO online services.

If your situation is more complex — multiple employers who withheld inconsistently, foreign income, or you think the Addy case treaty exception might apply to you — using a registered tax agent is a safer choice than self-lodging.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. Will I definitely get a tax refund as a working holiday maker?
Not automatically. You'll only receive a refund if the total tax withheld from your pay during the year was more than your final tax liability once calculated — for example, if an employer withheld at 30% by mistake, or you earned less than your withholding assumed. If your withholding matched your actual liability precisely, there's nothing to refund.
Q2. Can I claim the tax-free threshold or LITO as a working holiday maker?
No. Working holiday makers on 417/462 visas don't get the standard tax-free threshold or the Low Income Tax Offset — these concessions are reserved for ordinary Australian tax residents, and this is one of the most common misconceptions among first-time WHM lodgers.
Q3. Do I need to pay the Medicare Levy as a working holiday maker?
Generally, no. Working holiday makers do not pay the Medicare levy because they generally cannot access Medicare benefits, and foreign residents for tax purposes are exempt from the levy entirely. There is a narrow exception for tax residents from countries with a reciprocal health care agreement with Australia. Use the Medicare Levy toggle in the calculator above to see how this changes your estimate.
Q4. What happens to my superannuation when I leave Australia — and how much tax applies?
You can claim it back via a Departing Australia Superannuation Payment (DASP) once your visa has expired and you've left the country permanently. However, the ATO withholds 65% tax on the DASP payment, so you'll receive only 35% of your accumulated super balance — a much higher effective tax rate than the 15% you paid on your wages.

2025–26 完整指南:打工度假簽證(417/462)在澳洲的退稅攻略

本文內容已對照 2025–26 財政年度(2025 年 7 月 1 日至 2026 年 6 月 30 日)ATO 最新指引審閱。本文僅供一般資訊參考，並非個人化稅務建議——詳見本頁底部之法律免責聲明。

如果你持有 417 或 462 簽證，正在計算需要繳多少稅、或能退回多少稅，本篇指南將帶你了解確切稅率、幾乎每個人都會搞混的居民身分判定、合法提高退稅的方法，以及透過 myGov 報稅的完整步驟。

了解 2025/2026 財年的 WHM 稅率級距

澳洲於 2017 年針對打工度假者導入專屬稅制，俗稱「背包客稅」。與澳洲稅務居民不同，417 及 462 簽證持有人的前 $45,000 收入課徵 15%稅率，超過部分課徵 32.5%，再往上則適用更高的外國居民稅率。

最重要的觀念是:2025–26 及 2026–27 財年,417/462 簽證持有人的前 $45,000 收入一律課徵 15% 稅率，且無論你被認定為澳洲稅務居民或外國稅務居民，這項規則都同樣適用。這與一般居民不同——一般居民享有前 $18,200 完全免稅，但 WHM 簽證持有人完全沒有這項優惠。

15% 稅率能否正確適用，取決於雇主是否完成登記。雇用打工度假者的雇主必須向 ATO 登記，才能以 15% 稅率預扣稅款——若未登記，則必須以外國居民稅率預扣，也就是從第一塊錢開始就課徵 30%。建議直接向雇主確認，或檢查薪資單，以確認雇主是否已完成登記——這正是許多 WHM 工作者最終能拿到退稅的最常見原因之一。

ATO 提供的實例說明了實際運作方式:當已登記的雇主以週薪方式支付 WHM 工作者薪資時，稅款會從第一筆薪資就以 15% 預扣——例如週薪 $1,000，會被預扣 $150。全年累積下來，你就能理解為何預扣稅款會迅速累積，這也正是報稅核對最終數字如此重要的原因。

還有一點務必留意:若沒有稅號(TFN)，情況會更糟。雇主必須對未在到職 28 天內提供 TFN 的打工度假者，以 45% 稅率預扣款項——因此，在第一次領薪前盡快申請 TFN，應是抵達澳洲後最優先要做的事情之一。

你是稅務居民還是打工度假者?DASP 與居民身分完整說明

這是多數 WHM 簽證持有人最容易搞混的地方，我們先釐清兩個經常被混為一談的不同問題:你的稅務居民身分，以及你的 WHM 簽證身分。兩者會互相影響，但並不會互相抵消。

多數情況下，WHM 的居民身分並不會改變你的 15% 稅率。對大多數 WHM 而言，無論你是澳洲稅務居民或外國稅務居民，都不會影響你適用的稅率——WHM 專屬稅制在兩種情況下皆適用。唯一的例外，涉及澳洲租稅協定中特定「無歧視待遇條款(NDA)」的協定國，一項具指標性意義的高等法院判例改變了這個情況。

Addy 案例在此至關重要。在 Addy v Commissioner of Taxation [2021] HCA 34 一案中，高等法院認定，對同時具備澳洲稅務居民身分的英國打工度假者課徵背包客稅，違反了澳英雙重課稅協定中的無歧視待遇條款。實務上而言:若你來自具有無歧視待遇條款的協定國(常見的協定國包括英國、美國、德國、日本、智利、芬蘭、以色列、挪威及土耳其)，且你符合一般居民身分測試的資格，你可能有權適用一般居民稅階課稅——包含 $18,200 免稅門檻——而非一律適用 15% 稅率。這是一個相當複雜的領域，也是本指南中少數強烈建議在報稅前先諮詢註冊稅務代理人的情況。

對其他多數人而言，標準居民身分測試(住所測試、Domicile 測試、183 天測試、退休金測試)主要決定的是其他事項——例如你是否可能需要繳納 Medicare Levy，以及是否需要申報海外來源收入——而非你適用哪一種 WHM 稅率。

退休金與 DASP。退休金(Superannuation)的運作方式與所得稅完全不同。自 2025 年 7 月 1 日起，所有雇主都必須在薪資之外，額外提撥薪資的 12% 至退休金帳戶，而身為打工度假簽證持有者，你必須等到永久離開澳洲後，才能動用這筆退休金。簽證到期並離境後，你可以申請離境澳洲退休金給付(DASP)。但要注意:這筆款項的課稅方式與你的一般所得稅並不相同。申請 DASP 時，ATO 會扣除 65% 的稅款，僅退還餘額的 35% 給你——這項稅率自 2017 年 7 月 1 日起適用至今。65% 的稅率遠高於 15% 的所得稅率，因此規劃離境時間時，務必將這項差異納入考量。

如何合法最大化你的退稅

由於 15% 稅率從第一塊錢就開始課徵，且沒有免稅門檻，扣除額(Deductions)是少數你能掌控的槓桿之一。每一塊錢合法的工作相關支出，都能降低你的應稅所得——以 15% 稅率計算，每申報 1 元扣除額，就能換回 15 分退稅。以下是與 WHM 工作者真正相關的五大類扣除額:

  1. 工作地點間的交通費。若你同時從事多份臨時工作，或同一天內需直接往返兩個不同工作地點(而非從家中通勤的日常交通)，這類交通費用通常可以申報。建議簡單記錄日期、里程與行程目的。
  2. 未獲雇主補貼的防護服、制服與安全裝備。農場工作、餐飲服務業，以及與營建相關的職務，經常需要特定的安全鞋、高視度服裝、手套或防曬裝備，且雇主未提供補貼。務必保留收據——即使金額不大也會累積:若打工度假者申報 $500 的合法工作相關扣除額，即可降低 $500 應稅所得，以 15% 稅率計算等於少繳 $75 的稅。
  3. 與職務相關的自備工具與設備。若你的工作需要自備基本工具(例如園藝剪、廚房刀具組等)，這些費用——或較高價值物品的折舊——通常可以申報。
  4. 與目前工作直接相關的進修課程。與你目前工作內容直接相關的短期課程或證照(例如擔任咖啡師期間修習咖啡師課程，或餐飲業的 RSA 複訓)可能可以申報扣除額——但與未來完全不同職涯方向相關的課程，通常不可申報。
  5. 工會或專業協會會費(若你是與目前工作相關組織的會員)，以及申報前一年度報稅時支付的稅務代理人費用。

黃金原則:你只能申報實際發生、與賺取收入直接相關、且有憑證可查的支出。ATO 的資料比對系統日益精密，過度申報可能觸發審查——因此,「最大化退稅」的正確做法永遠是把你應得的扣除額申報完整，而非憑空捏造支出。

myGov 報稅逐步教學

你可以透過連結 ATO 的 myGov 帳號，免費自行報稅。以下是完整流程:

  1. 建立或登入 myGov 帳號。前往 my.gov.au，建立新帳號或登入現有帳號。
  2. 將 ATO 服務連結至 myGov 帳號。在「Services」項目中選擇「Link a service」，並選擇 Australian Taxation Office。你需要提供部分身分驗證資訊(TFN、銀行帳戶資料或所得申報書)以完成連結。
  3. 等待所得資料顯示「Tax ready」。雇主會透過 Single Touch Payroll 系統，直接向 ATO 申報你的薪資與已扣繳稅款。這項資訊通常會在財年結束(6 月 30 日)後數週內顯示於你的 myGov 帳號中。建議等待此狀態出現後再報稅，因為事後修改申報書會比稍作等待更麻煩。
  4. 開始你的報稅程序。在 ATO 線上服務首頁，依序選擇「Tax」→「Lodgments」→「Income tax」，並點選對應年度旁的「Prepare」。
  5. 核對系統預填資料。你的收入與已扣繳稅款應會依所得申報書自動預填。若你有多位雇主，請確認所有雇主資料皆已顯示。
  6. 填入扣除額。在「Deductions」項目中，手動輸入前述提及的工作相關扣除額。
  7. 核對、聲明並送出申報。系統會在你送出前計算預估退稅(或應繳金額)。請仔細核對摘要內容，確認聲明後送出申報。
  8. 追蹤退稅進度。多數電子申報案件約在兩週內處理完成，你可在 ATO 線上服務的「Manage tax returns」頁面追蹤進度。

若你的情況較為複雜——例如多位雇主預扣稅率不一致、擁有海外收入，或認為自己可能適用 Addy 案例的協定豁免——委託註冊稅務代理人協助申報，會比自行報稅更為保險。

常見問題 FAQ

Q1. 身為打工度假者，我一定會拿到退稅嗎?
不一定。只有當雇主全年預扣的稅額「超過」你最終應繳的稅額時，才會有退稅——例如雇主誤用 30% 稅率預扣，或你實際收入低於預扣時的估算基準。若預扣金額剛好等於應繳稅額，則沒有退稅可拿。
Q2. 身為打工度假者，我可以申請免稅門檻或 LITO 嗎?
不行。417/462 簽證持有人無法適用一般居民的免稅門檻($18,200)或低收入抵稅額(LITO)，這兩項優惠僅限一般澳洲稅務居民，是新手報稅時最常見的誤解之一。
Q3. 身為打工度假者，我需要繳納 Medicare Levy 嗎?
一般來說不需要。多數 WHM 因無法使用 Medicare 醫療福利，因此免繳 2% 的 Medicare Levy，且外國稅務居民本就完全豁免此項目。唯一例外是來自與澳洲簽有「互惠醫療協議」國家、且被認定為稅務居民者。可於上方計算機切換 Medicare Levy 選項，查看金額差異。
Q4. 離開澳洲後，我的退休金會怎麼處理?需要繳多少稅?
簽證到期且永久離境後，可透過 DASP(Departing Australia Superannuation Payment)申請退回退休金，但 ATO 會對這筆款項預扣 65% 的稅，實際只退還 35% 給你——這個稅率遠高於工作時 15% 的所得稅率，規劃離境時務必將這筆稅負差異納入考量。
